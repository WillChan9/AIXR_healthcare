ICU Single-Patient Room — Character-centric, equipment-dense

A small but equipment-dense intensive-care room centered on a single
critically ill patient. The patient bed is the focal point at the
center-back of the room, surrounded by monitoring equipment (multiple
wall-mounted vital-signs monitors), a ventilator, an IV drip stand,
and an infusion pump. A dedicated ICU nurse attends closely at the
bedside. The lighting is dim and clinical with one focused light over
the bed. Atmosphere is tense, quiet, and precise — every device is
positioned so the nurse can reach it without leaving the bedside.

[interactions]
Touching the nurse should make her speak quietly, describing the
patient's status (e.g., "stable but sedated", "responding to treatment").
Touching any wall-mounted monitor should make the nurse read out the
relevant vital reading aloud. Touching the ventilator or infusion pump
should describe its function and current setting. Touching the patient
should produce only a faint moan — he is sedated; the nurse instead
speaks on his behalf.

A short story arc: touching the patient → vitals on the wall monitor
highlight as the nurse explains they're checking his readings. Touching
the highlighted monitor afterward → the nurse walks one step closer to
the bed and adjusts a setting. Touching the IV drip finally → the nurse
adjusts the drip rate and the lighting brightens momentarily as if a
crisis has passed.
